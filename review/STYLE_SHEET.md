# Style sheet: Dunant register

Derived from two first-author papers, analysed 2026-09-11:
- Dunant (2021), *Are We Missing the Target? A Bias-Variance Perspective on Multi-Hazard Risk Assessment*, Frontiers in Earth Science 9:685301. A Perspective: argumentative, didactic, built on one carried analogy.
- Dunant et al. (2025), *Impacts from cascading multi-hazards using hypergraphs: a case study from the 2015 Gorkha earthquake in Nepal*, NHESS 25:267. A research paper: measured, methodologically humble.

**Target register for the review: NHESS.** First person for methodological and interpretive choices, passive for description and results. No organising analogy (standing instruction: the bias-variance paper is style-only, and a carried analogy sits too close to content).

## Sentence construction
- Typical 15–35 words. Mix simple declaratives with subordinated explanatory structures; do not write only one or the other.
- Split anything above ~45 words. The pre-restyle manuscript had a 90th percentile of 55 and a maximum of 113.
- Simple sentences carry definitions and openings ("A graph comprises a set of nodes connected by edges"). Subordination carries the reasoning.

## Person and voice
- `we` / `our` for methodological and interpretive choices: *Here we treat*, *We apply*, *We take*, *We found no*, *We stress that*, *We hesitate to*.
- Passive and impersonal for description, measurement and results.
- Target density roughly 15–25 instances of `we`/`our` per 10,000 words. The pre-restyle manuscript had 5.2 combined.

## Connectives
Restore explicit signposting at genuine turns. Characteristic set: **However, Despite, Thus, Therefore, In contrast, Similarly, In sum** (with *Notably* and *Importantly* used sparingly, see the narrative rule below). The pre-restyle manuscript contained none of these.
Use at real turns only; do not sprinkle. Roughly one per two or three paragraphs.

## Signposting and orientation
- Orient through the argument itself, not through a roadmap. A section opens with its first claim; the reader should see where the section is going from what that claim leaves open.
- *Below we…* and *We close by…* only in the Introduction's structure paragraph and in methods. The v22 pass used 21 *Below we…* openers; the v23 narrative pass removed them because they read as a table of contents repeated inside each section.

## Narrative, not a list of arguments (user instruction, 2026-10-06)
The paper must read as a connected narrative, not as a sequence of enumerated points.
- No counted announcements: *Three features…*, *Two conclusions follow*, *Our argument has four parts*, *Risk requires three terms*.
- No ordinal scaffolding: *First,… Second,… Finally,…*, *The first is… The second is…*, *The third constraint…*.
- Join paragraphs by the logic that connects them: cause (*because*, *so*), consequence (*It follows that*), contrast (*by contrast*, *yet*), scale or sequence in the physical process (*Once a fire is alight*, *Further downstream*, *Above the level of the incident*).
- The "What differs in mountains" subsections follow the hazard chain or the governance chain, each paragraph handing over to the next; no paragraph opens with a bald standalone claim.
- Keep *Notably*, *Importantly*, *Crucially* and *We stress* rare (one or two in the whole paper); they announce emphasis instead of earning it.
- Max 5 subsections per section, everywhere.

## Hedging, calibrated to claim strength
- Strong where evidence is strong; modal where it is not.
- Vocabulary: *may, could, likely, appears to, tends to, suggests, is plausibly*.
- Modal density rises in the discussion and falls in the results. Speculation is marked, not smuggled.
- Soften negative findings rather than dramatising them (the NHESS paper writes "tendency towards over-prediction", not "the model fails").

## Paragraph openings
Vary them. Four patterns, roughly balanced:
1. **Assertion**: *A graph comprises a set of nodes.*
2. **Problem-posed**: *There remain, however, some important challenges and limitations.*
3. **Linking**: the opening picks up what the previous paragraph left unresolved (*Once a fire is alight the departure widens…*; *How long the threshold stays lowered is set by…*).
4. **Contrastive**: *Despite its advantages, however, the network model suffers…*
Avoid opening more than about a fifth of paragraphs with "The". The pre-restyle manuscript opened 113 of 271 that way.

## Numbers and citations
- Numbers embedded as grammatical units, never in apposition: *the 2015 Mw 7.8 Gorkha earthquake*, not *the Gorkha earthquake (Mw 7.8, 2015)*.
- Citations parenthetical, mid-sentence or clause-final. Never narrative ("the work of X shows").
- Quantifiers precede: *approximately*, *about*, *of the order of*.

## Uncertainty and limitations
- Both a dedicated treatment and inline caveats.
- Characteristic constructions: *We stress that this layer is used simply as an exemplar*; *note that this measure is likely to under-predict*; *We hesitate to draw conclusions from a single event*.
- Graduated rather than binary: *much less well at smaller scales*, not *does not work*.
- Acknowledge without defensiveness, then redirect to what would resolve it.

## Headings
2–10 words, descriptive noun phrases or gerunds. Colons for subtopics. No interrogatives in the review (they belong to the Perspective register).

## Carried over from earlier instructions, unchanged
- No em dashes for parentheticals; parentheses or commas.
- No `\citet`, no narrative citation, no direct quotation of sources.
- No bullet or numbered lists in the discussion or conclusions.
- Where a claim rests on fewer than three independent cases, state the number.
- Mark imported mechanisms as imported at the point of use.
- No self-promotional novelty claims.
