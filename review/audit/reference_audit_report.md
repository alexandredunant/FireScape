# Reference audit: *What Makes Mountain Wildfire Different?* (manuscript v5)

Audit date: 18–19 October 2026. Manuscript audited: `review/review_paper_v5.tex` with `FireScape_master_v6.bib`. Corrections are carried in `review_paper_v6.tex` and `FireScape_master_v7.bib`; v5 and v6 of the bibliography are unchanged.

## Method

Every citation command in the manuscript (`\citep`, `\citealp`) was expanded to one record per cited key and line, giving **646 citations of 150 sources**. Each record holds the citing sentence, the bibliography entry and the source PDF in `review/papers/`. Six reviewers each took 25 sources and, for every citation, searched the full text of the source for the passage that supports or contradicts the sentence, checking every number, place and count attributed to it. Bibliographic details were checked against the PDF and against Crossref (DataCite or doi.org where Crossref has no record).

Verdicts: **SUPPORTED** (the source states or clearly shows the claim); **CONTEXT_OK** (a background pointer to a source on the right topic); **PARTIAL** (mostly right, but a number, place, scale or strength of claim is off); **NOT_IN_SOURCE** (no support after a genuine search); **WRONG_SOURCE** (the claim belongs to another paper).

The most consequential findings were re-read in the PDFs by the lead reviewer (`lead_reviewer_checks.csv`). A second, blind reviewer re-judged every NOT_IN_SOURCE and WRONG_SOURCE flag and a random 10% of SUPPORTED verdicts (see the last section).

## What the problems have in common

Most PARTIAL verdicts are fixable wording rather than unsupported science, but they fall into a few recurring patterns that a *Reviews of Geophysics* referee would notice:

- **Scale inflation.** A result from one valley, one site, one country or one fire is written as a property of "the Alps" or of "mountains" (e.g. Rey et al. 2019, two sites in one Valais valley; Moser et al., one dry-valley burn; Stoof et al. 2024, the Netherlands generalised to lowland Europe; Gerberding et al. 2025, Alpine Space percentages that include about 47% lowland).
- **Pooled results attributed to the Alps.** Beloiu et al. 2026's ~72 m/decade upslope shift is pooled over eight ranges and led by the Balkans, Iberia and Turkey; the Alps show only limited upslope expansion.
- **Hedged findings hardened.** "May", "suggests" and "reasonably reduced" in the source become firm statements (e.g. La Porta et al. 2025, cited six times; Rangwala; Gorriz-Mifsud).
- **Imported mechanisms not marked as imported.** Evidence from Colorado, California, Canada, Greece or Iberia is used for the Alps without saying so, against the manuscript's own transfer rule (e.g. Aubry-Wake et al. 2022, one Canadian glacier).
- **Statements the source does not make.** Most notably Forrest et al. 2024, cited five times (Table 2, Sections 3–4, Discussion) for limited skill in mountains, which the paper never states.
- **Numbers attached to the wrong quantity.** E.g. a model grid read as a product resolution (Forrest), a validation filter read as a detection limit (Perello et al. 2025), raster-cell counts read as tree counts (Frei et al. 2023).
