Dear Editors,

We would like to propose a review article for *Reviews of Geophysics* titled *Wildfires in the European Alps: An Interdisciplinary Review*. The attached proposal gives the title, the potential authors, the scientific questions, the abstract and outline, an analysis of recent similar reviews, and how the journal's readership would benefit.

Wildfire science in Europe has been built mainly around large summer fires in the Mediterranean. Fire is now moving upslope and northwards into the Alps, where most fires burn less than a hectare and many forests protect settlements and roads from rockfall, avalanches and landslides. In this setting the damage from a fire does not scale with the area burnt, and several standard tools (fire-danger indices, spread models, satellite fire products) lose reliability. The review brings together the Alpine evidence on fire hazard, climate and weather, hydrology, forest dynamics, risk and governance. It is evidence-graded: each section opens with the trends that several independent studies support, and findings that rest on a single case are reported as such.

We believe the review suits the journal's broad readership. It connects three recent *Reviews of Geophysics* syntheses that do not yet meet on mountain fire risk: Jones et al. (2022) on global fire trends, Pepin et al. (2022) on elevation-dependent climate change, and Mani et al. (2023) on high-mountain process chains. It is written for geoscientists outside fire science, with technical terms defined at first use and a glossary.

The author team at Eurac Research (Bolzano, Italy) covers climate and disaster risk, climatology, Earth observation, water-related risk, forest ecology, ecosystem services and forest economics. A complete draft (about 13,500 words, 136 cited sources) already exists, so we could submit a polished manuscript well within three months of an invitation.

Thank you for considering this proposal. We would be glad to adjust its scope or emphasis in light of the Board's advice.
