Dear Editors,

We would like to propose a review article for Reviews of Geophysics titled "Wildfires in the European Alps: An Interdisciplinary Review". The attached proposal gives the title, the potential authors, the scientific questions, the abstract and outline, an analysis of recent similar reviews, and how the journal's readership would benefit - as per the journal requirements.

Fire management in Europe has developed mainly around large summer fires in the Mediterranean, where most of Europe's burnt area lies. Burning in Europe is now shifting northwards and, in mountains, upslope, and fire hazard is projected to rise across the Alps, which makes more frequent large fires plausible. Today most Alpine fires still burn less than a hectare, and many forests protect settlements and roads from rockfall, avalanches and landslides. In this setting, where a fire burns may matter as much for its consequences as how large it is, and several standard tools (e.g. fire-danger indices, spread models, satellite fire products) can lose reliability. The review brings together the Alpine evidence on fire hazard, climate and weather, hydrology, forest dynamics, risk and governance, drawing on the expertise of the author team at Eurac Research.

We believe the review suits the journal's broad readership. Three recent Reviews of Geophysics articles approach the topic from different sides: Jones et al. (2022) call for more work on how topography affects fire spread, Pepin et al. (2022) note that warming may push forest fire lines upslope, and Mani et al. (2023) classify high-mountain hazard chains that do not yet include fire. Our review addresses mountain wildfire directly. It is written for geoscientists outside fire science, with technical terms defined at first use and a glossary.

A complete draft (about 13,700 words, 138 cited sources) already exists, so we can submit a polished manuscript within three months of an invitation.

Thank you for considering this proposal. We would be glad to adjust its scope or emphasis in light of the Board's advice.

With kind regards,
