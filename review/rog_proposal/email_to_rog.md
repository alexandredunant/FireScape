Dear Editors,

We would like to propose a review article for Reviews of Geophysics titled "Wildfires in the European Alps: An Interdisciplinary Review". The attached proposal gives the title, the potential authors, the scientific questions, the abstract and outline, an analysis of recent similar reviews, and how the journal's readership would benefit - as per the journal requirements.

Fire management in Europe has developed mainly around large summer fires in the Mediterranean, where most of Europe's burnt area lies. Burning in Europe is now shifting northwards and, in mountains, upslope, and fire hazard is projected to rise across the Alps, where most fires burn less than a hectare and many forests protect settlements and roads from rockfall, avalanches and landslides. In this setting, where a fire burns may matter more for its consequences than how large it is, and several standard tools (e.g. fire-danger indices, spread models, satellite fire products) can lose reliability. The review brings together the Alpine evidence on fire hazard, climate and weather, hydrology, forest dynamics, risk and governance, drawing on the expertise of the author team at Eurac Research.

We believe the review suits the journal's broad readership. It connects three recent Reviews of Geophysics syntheses that do not yet meet on mountain fire risk: Jones et al. (2022) on global fire trends, Pepin et al. (2022) on elevation-dependent climate change, and Mani et al. (2023) on high-mountain process chains. It is written for geoscientists outside fire science, with technical terms defined at first use and a glossary.

A complete draft (about 13,500 words, 136 cited sources) already exists, so we can submit a polished manuscript within three months of an invitation.

Thank you for considering this proposal. We would be glad to adjust its scope or emphasis in light of the Board's advice.

With kind regards,
